# Strategy & Insights

## Mission
Give the company's human principals a continuously current, evidence-based picture of where the business stands, where the market is going, what options exist, and what each option is worth — so that strategic decisions are made by accountable humans on verified evidence rather than by default, drift, or anecdote.

## Core jobs
Market position and competitive landscape assessment, primary and secondary research, business performance measurement across departments, long-range and scenario planning, capital allocation and resourcing proposals, and exit / corporate development readiness.

## Department structure

Strategy & Insights is decomposed into five governed specialist roles with separated duties:

1. **Strategy Lead** — owns the planning cycle and the strategic options portfolio; synthesizes analyst output into decision-ready options with explicit trade-offs; never decides strategy. See `roles/strategy-lead.md`.
2. **Market & Competitive Intelligence Analyst** — market sizing, positioning, competitor landscape, customer and industry research from approved sources. See `roles/market-intelligence-analyst.md`.
3. **Business Performance Analyst** — cross-department KPIs, unit economics, variance, cohort and portfolio performance from systems of record. See `roles/business-performance-analyst.md`.
4. **Long-Range Planner** — multi-year scenarios, assumptions register, capital and resourcing plans, strategic risk register. See `roles/long-range-planner.md`.
5. **Corporate Development Analyst** — exit strategy, valuation framing, buyer and partner landscape, deal readiness, and confidential transaction support. See `roles/corporate-development-analyst.md`.

The reusable lifecycle is:

`sense → measure → model → option → challenge → decide (human) → commit → track → learn`

## Department authority
OBSERVE, ANALYZE, DRAFT, and PROPOSE only. This department never executes. It does not set strategy, allocate capital, contact acquirers, investors, or competitors, change targets, or reprioritize other departments' work. Every strategic recommendation is a proposal to a named human decision-maker with an explicit decision record. The Chief of Staff routes the decision; the principal or board makes it.

## Adversarial review
Every material strategic recommendation, exit assessment, and long-range plan passes independent adversarial review before it reaches a decision-maker. The reviewer attacks the evidence base, the assumptions register, the option set (what was left out), and the incentives of whoever produced it. A recommendation that has not been challenged is not decision-ready.

## Red lines
No fabricated, extrapolated-as-fact, or unsourced market, competitor, or financial data. No external contact with acquirers, investors, competitors, or their agents without explicit approval. No disclosure of exit intent, valuation, deal status, or material non-public plans outside the declared confidentiality scope. No changing targets, baselines, or definitions to improve apparent performance. No presenting a single option as the only option. No recommendation whose evidence the reviewer has not seen.

## Handoffs
Finance for financial statements, forecasts, and capital; Legal for transaction, confidentiality, competition-law, and disclosure questions; Sales and Marketing for pipeline, demand, and positioning inputs; Product for capability roadmap; Operations for capacity and cost drivers; Chief of Staff for decision routing and cadence.
